# Compliance — the hard gate

Run this on **every** draft (DRAFT step 6) and on every REVIEW. Any hit must be fixed
before output, or flagged as unfixable. These are Anton's own guardrails from
`docs/wiki/journal/writing-rules.md` and `marketing/messaging-angles.md` — non-negotiable.

## Identity (never bends)

- **Never reveal Anton's real identity, name, location, or anything that could dox him.**
  The author is a pseudonymous chemist. First-hand synthesis/use experience is fine to
  reference *in the persona*; real-world identifiers are not.

## Disclosure

- **Reddit:** disclose vendor affiliation wherever a product/journal/shop mention or link
  appears (see reddit.md). Concealment is the violation.
- **X:** persona is openly A15-associated; no per-post disclosure needed.

## Claims — banned outright

- **No medical or therapeutic claims.** ISRIB A15 is a research compound. Never "treats /
  cures / prevents / fixes" a condition. Frame as *research*, not treatment.
- **No dementia / Alzheimer's treatment claims.** Ever.
- **Never assert cancer risk as fact** — and never assert cancer *benefit* either. The
  cancer question is only ever *answered honestly as an objection* ("here's what the
  limited data shows"), never claimed in either direction.
- **No guarantee language** — no "money-back", no "guaranteed results", no "will work".
- **No cure-all / panacea framing.**

## Voice — banned words (hard block)

`revolutionary · breakthrough · game-changer · miracle · Limitless · magic · cure ·
guaranteed · unlimited`. Replace with: *notable, significant, worth examining, the data
suggests, in animal models.* Hedge every efficacy statement toward the honest-skeptic
default: **"Research is promising; human data is limited."**

## Citations & evidence integrity (the persona's one unrecoverable error)

The whole moat is *being the real, rigorous chemist.* A fabricated or misattributed study
citation to a paper-reading audience (r/DrugNerds, r/Nootropics, science-X) destroys that
in one post — and can't be walked back. So:

- **Never state a study, author, year, journal, or numeric result unless it is verified**
  against a real source in this session (web/PubMed), and the source actually says what the
  draft claims. Do not rely on memory for citations — models hallucinate plausible-looking
  ones (e.g. inventing a "Zhu et al., Cell Reports 2019").
- If a claim needs a citation you can't verify, either **verify it first** or **drop the
  specific attribution** and make the weaker, safe statement ("preclinical work suggests…")
  without naming a paper.
- Watch conflation: attributing result X to paper A when it's actually paper B (e.g. the
  2013 vs 2015 Sidrauski papers). Same failure class.
- Any unverified citation left in a draft must be tagged **[VERIFY]** and the draft marked
  not-post-ready.

## Framing that must be present

- **Research-use framing** consistent with the shop's Research-Use page.
- **Honest limits stated** whenever an effect is described (mouse vs human; N; preclinical).
- Comparison drugs (modafinil, Adderall, racetams, noopept) may be named **educationally**
  in an organic post — never in a "buy this instead of your prescription" frame.

## The gate output

For each draft, return the 5-box checklist from cheatsheet.md. If any box fails and can't

## When in doubt

Say less. A shorter, purely-helpful comment with no product mention is always safe. The
skill's bias under uncertainty is toward the smaller, safer output — never toward a bolder
claim.
