## The 6 beliefs required to buy

1. **The problem is biological, not personal.** Brain fog / memory / motivation loss
   come from a biological block, not laziness or weak discipline. Reframe: "my brain
   is stuck in a protective stress response — a fixable state."
   *Copy angle:* "Your brain isn't broken. It's stuck. There's a difference."

2. **The ISR is the root cause, and it's reversible.** ISR is an internal brake on
   cognition under chronic stress/aging/burnout — and it's reversible. The logical
   bridge: "if the problem is ISR, and ISRIB targets ISR directly, this might
   actually work." Key proof point: rapid effects in aged mice showed age-related
   cognitive loss can be a *reversible* blockage, not permanent degradation.

3. **ISRIB A15 is categorically different from other nootropics.** Not "just another
   stack" — it reboots protein synthesis via eIF2B, a different mechanism from
   everything they've tried (modafinil, amphetamines, racetams, Bacopa/Lion's Mane,
   noopept, Qualia/Alpha Brain). *Angle:* "You've tried what modulates your
   neurotransmitters. You haven't tried what releases the cellular brake on protein
   synthesis itself."

4. **Real people like me have used it and gotten results.** Not just mouse studies —
   sophisticated biohackers self-administered A15 and reported concrete memory /
   clarity / focus / motivation gains.

5. **It's safe, non-stimulant, no crashes or anxiety.** No stimulant side effects, no
   dependence, remarkable animal safety. Walter: "We have never seen any relevant
   side effects. None. It is totally benign." *Angle:* "Not a stimulant. Not a hack.
   A targeted molecular intervention — synthesized by a pharmaceutical chemist,
   NMR-verified, backed by UCSF research."

6. **This is my opportunity, and I deserve it.** Trying A15 isn't reckless — it's
   aligned with their optimizer identity. The brain is the final frontier. *Angle:*
   "You've optimized your sleep, training, nutrition. One biological pathway has been
   quietly capping everything else."
