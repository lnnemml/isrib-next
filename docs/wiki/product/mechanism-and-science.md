## Mechanism in four sentences

Under stress (misfolded proteins, injury, aging), eIF2alpha is phosphorylated and
inactivates eIF2B, halting general protein synthesis — the Integrated Stress
Response. Chronic ISR activation keeps that brake on, and memory formation needs new
protein synthesis. ISRIB binds at the interface of two eIF2B subunits and stabilizes
its active form, letting translation continue despite stress signals. Net effect:
the cellular brake on memory formation is released — restoration, not stimulation.

## Evidence headlines (to be expanded with citations on ingest)

- Enhanced learning/memory in healthy mice (Sidrauski et al., 2013).
- Reversed TBI cognitive deficits months post-injury (Rosi & Walter, 2017);
  normalized dendritic spines; benefits persisted after dosing stopped.
- Reversed age-related decline in aged mice (2020, eLife) — the single most
  conversion-relevant claim: cognitive loss as a *reversible blockage*.
- Corrected Down syndrome / Fragile X model deficits.
- Well-tolerated in animals; "totally benign" (Walter). Human trials only beginning
  (Calico ALS trial, 2024).

## A15 specifics

More potent analog (dichlorophenoxy substitution); ~5–15 mg oral; effects reported
"identical but stronger" vs original ISRIB; capsule form removes the DMSO/solubility
problem of the parent molecule.
